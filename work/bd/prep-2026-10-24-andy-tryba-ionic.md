# Coffee Prep — Andy Tryba, Ionic Partners (Austin)

**What this is:** an advice coffee with a friend from your Bible study, not a pitch and not a raise. Same feedback-first rule as with pastors: ask for wisdom, and the right doors open on their own.
**Status:** not asked yet. First real name in the warm path (tracker row 21).
**Prepared:** Oct 24, 2026, from public web sources (links at the bottom). Titles shift; confirm in conversation, don't recite his resume to him.

## How to ask (in person, after Bible study, or a text)
> "Hey Andy, I found out what you do and I'd love your honest read on something I've been building for churches. Not asking for anything, just your perspective on the business side. Could I buy you a coffee sometime in the next couple weeks?"

Keep it out of the Bible study itself. One ask, then let him pick the time.

## Who he is (public sources)
- **Co-founder and managing partner, Ionic Partners** (Austin, founded 2020) with Donald Park (formerly of Vista Equity Partners).
- **Ionic's focus:** buying and turning around enterprise software companies that are past startup but stuck. They call it the "Second Chasm," roughly **$20–50M in revenue**. They run them with a cloud-first, global remote workforce.
- **Ionic's deals:** Gigster (bought 2021, sold to Virtasant 2024) · CXT Software (courier and last-mile software, bought Feb 2025; Andy is listed as its CEO) · CXT + e-Courier combination (late 2025).
- **Before Ionic:** built and ran a ~$1B division (Think3) at ESW Capital · **founded Crossover**, a remote talent marketplace · **helped create RideAustin**, a local *nonprofit* rideshare.

## What this means for you
- **He is not your investor.** Ionic buys established companies with tens of millions in revenue. Don't let either of you feel the meeting is about money.
- **He *is* a rare advisor** for three things you need:
  1. **Software business judgment.** He's fixed the economics of many software companies. Pricing, renewals, margins, what makes a church SaaS fundable or not.
  2. **How to get the product built.** Crossover and Gigster are both about building software with remote talent. That's directly relevant to your open seat: technical co-founder vs. a contracted dev team vs. a hybrid. Ask him.
  3. **Network.** Austin angels, faith-driven investors, operators, possibly engineers.
- **Personal hook, only if it's natural:** RideAustin was a local, community-first nonprofit. He's built something for a community before.

## The three demo beats (phone; say "sample data" up front)
1. **The whole member app in 60 seconds.** One app for a member's church life: needs and help (Rachel's roofer), groups as their home base, the church calendar and announcements, messaging staff. Keep it short; he'll care more about the business than the screens.
2. **Insights: why the church pays.** Year in Review, gifts census, newcomer funnel, care signals (said precisely: aggregate except one, activity dates only).
3. **The moat.** Groups-as-headquarters switching costs + the "no algorithm, no ads, no gamification" posture that engagement-driven competitors can't copy.

## Numbers to have ready (honest, from the plan)
- **Pricing (hypothesis, not validated):** Small ≤500 $4,500 · Medium ≤1,000 $7,500 · Large ≤2,000 $12,000 · XL custom/yr. Members free.
- **Market:** ~384,000 U.S. churches. Target is 500+ members, sweet spot 1,000–2,000.
- **Plan:** 5–10 signed founding-church LOIs → ~$150K to build the MVP and launch ~10 pilots in 12 months (to be firmed up with dev quotes) → seed and/or technical co-founder.
- **Unit-economics targets** (targets, not results): 85–90% gross margin · CAC payback under 3 months · $15–40K church lifetime value over 5 years.
- **Where you actually are:** prototype live; 27 cold emails, 2 replies, 0 meetings held yet; recovery drafts ready. Say it plainly. Someone who turns around companies will respect a founder who knows his real numbers.

## Questions to ask him (pick 3–4; listen more than talk)
1. "Is this a venture-scale business, or one I should bootstrap? What would make it one or the other?"
2. "Do these prices feel right for what you just saw? How would you test them?"
3. "Technical co-founder, contracted team, or both? With your Crossover and Gigster experience, how would you get an MVP built for ~$150K?"
4. "What would you need to see before an investor takes this seriously: how many LOIs, pilots, renewals?"
5. "What worries you most about selling to churches?"
6. Close: **"Who else should see this?"** (an angel, a faith-driven fund, an operator, an engineer). Get names *and* permission to use his.

## Likely pushback → honest answers
- **"Churches are slow, small-budget buyers."** True, which is why we sell to 500+ member churches that already spend five figures on tools, and why the pastor's endorsement is the channel.
- **"Why won't Planning Center just add this?"** Their business is staff administration. Member-to-member connection without ads or algorithms is a different product and culture. We complement them, and integration is on the roadmap.
- **"Cold start: why would members join?"** A Launch Sunday into a congregation that already trusts its pastor. That's our expectation, not yet a result; pilots will prove it or not.
- **"You're a solo, non-technical founder."** Yes. That's the open seat, and exactly why I wanted your read (question 3).

## Don't
- Don't ask for money or an intro to Ionic's fund.
- Don't overclaim. The demo inventory (`work/product/Dex_Demo_Inventory_for_Sonny.md`) is the line between what's shown and what's roadmap.

## After
Debrief within the hour (`skills/debrief.md`), by voice memo is fine: what he thought, his answers to your questions, any names he offered, and the next step. Tell any session and it gets logged.

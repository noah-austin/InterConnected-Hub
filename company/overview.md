## What it is
A private, invitation-only community platform sold to individual churches. Each congregation gets one network, fully siloed, joined through church-distributed codes. Members discover each other's skills, gifts, and needs during the week. When someone needs a roofer, a tutor, or babysitting help, the first thought becomes someone at church instead of Google.

Church software serves the staff. This serves the body.

## The two-sided value proposition (the spine of every message)
- **Members join for community.** Needs met, gifts discovered, Sunday relationships becoming weekday family.
- **The church pays for community plus insight.** It gets the first real visibility any church has had into its own body: a gifts and skills census, member map and growth geography, a new-member integration funnel, group gap analysis, pastoral care signals, and a Year in Review for vision night.

Privacy, stated precisely: every insight is aggregate except one. Quiet-member care signals are individual-level and use activity dates only, never content. Message content is never analyzed. Confidential circles never appear in admin views or insights. Members are never ranked.

## The product vision test: member-complete, not staff-complete
Interconnected aims to be the one app a church member needs during the week. That means the congregation's front door, built on a connection layer nobody else has. If a member needs it during their week, it belongs. If it is a staff workflow (volunteer scheduling, check-ins, giving administration, kids registration), it stays with existing church management software until the member is won. We complement Planning Center, Subsplash and Breeze; we do not replace them.

What a member does in the app: find or offer help (feed) · find people (search) · belong to something smaller (groups) · talk privately (messages) · know what's happening (church page: announcements, events, calendar) · know who leads (leadership directory).

## The deliberate no's (the product's identity, and part of the moat)
No reputation scores or gamification · no algorithmic feed · no pressure mechanics · no transactional mentor-matching · member-led groups with admin oversight only · aggregate-only insights · community moderation rather than AI moderation. Full list with owners: `decisions.md`.

Annual B2B SaaS licensed to churches, priced by congregation size. **Members always join free.**

## Target market
- **Customer:** mid-to-large churches of 500+ members, sweet spot 1,000 to 2,000, suburban and urban, spending five figures a year on engagement and tech tools.
- **Personas:** connections, groups and community pastors own the problem and are the best first conversation at larger churches. Executive pastors own operations and budget. Lead pastors decide at smaller churches.
- **Initial geography:** the South Austin / Buda / Kyle / San Marcos corridor, extending to Dripping Springs, Wimberley, Lockhart, Manchaca and Oak Hill. This is Noah's home turf, and he can reach it in person.
- **Market context (always attributed):** 205M+ American Christians (Pew Research) · ~384,000 U.S. churches · 70% of U.S. Christians prefer values-aligned businesses (Barna Group).

## Go-to-market
- **The pastor is the channel.** Adoption rides pastoral trust: a Launch Sunday (pulpit announcement, slides, QR codes) into a pre-existing high-trust community. The working expectation is that half a congregation can join in the first weeks. That is an **expectation, never a statistic**. It never appears in outreach as a number.
- **Feedback-first.** Outreach asks for pastoral wisdom, not a pilot, and says plainly that nothing is being sold: "Ask for money, get advice; ask for advice, get money." This is also simply honest at prototype stage.
- **The no-link rule.** The demo URL is never sent in cold outreach. Noah shows it in person, with the member app on his phone and the dashboard on a larger screen. The demo is the reason to meet.
- **Every meeting** gets a prep sheet before and a debrief after, and ends with a referral ask. Objections are logged and become the FAQ and the roadmap.
- **The path to revenue** runs through founding-church letters of intent: one page, non-binding, with indicative pricing acknowledged. They are introduced only after a meeting has gone well, never in first contact.
- **Engines, in order of expected yield:** Austin Ridge (Noah's own church, which Noah handles personally) · referrals · multipliers (people who know many pastors) · cold second ring. The warm path (Noah's own network of business owners) runs in parallel throughout.
- **Parked until pilots exist:** denominational networks, conferences, referral programs, content marketing.

## Moat
1. Every user is a verified member of a shared church body. No spam, no strangers.
2. The deliberate exclusions cannot be copied by attention-economy platforms without breaking their business models.
3. **Groups as headquarters** creates ministry-level switching costs. Once a Bible study's notes, reading plans, files and sign-ups live on the platform, and the calendar and years of answered prayers accumulate there, leaving means unwinding the church's own weekly rhythms.

## Funding path (proof before asking)
1. Five to ten signed, non-binding founding-church LOIs.
2. A precisely defined ask: working frame about $150,000 to build and launch the MVP with roughly ten pilot churches in twelve months, to be firmed up with development quotes. A technical co-founder taking equity changes this substantially; both paths run at once.
3. Capital sources in order: warm circles → faith-driven investor ecosystem (Faith Driven Investor and Entrepreneur, Praxis, OCEAN) → angels via pastor referrals.
4. SAFE notes expected. A startup lawyer reviews every document before any signature or check. Nothing the company produces is legal or investment advice.

Detailed financial projections are deliberately deferred until field pricing and development quotes exist.

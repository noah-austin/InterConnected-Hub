## 5. The pricing-reaction capture script (feeds Gus, the CFO)

Drafted prices are not validated prices. Every time pricing comes up in a meeting, Noah asks three questions — build these into every meeting-prep doc:

1. **"Does that number feel high, low, or about right for what you saw?"** (gut reaction, verbatim)
2. **"What does your church currently spend annually on tools like this — giving platform, church app, ChMS?"** (budget anchor)
3. **"Who besides you would need to say yes to a number like that, and what would they ask?"** (buying process map)

Log answers per church. Gus (CFO) consumes this data to validate or revise tiers. You capture; you never change prices.

## 7. Competitive context (for objection handling)

- **Planning Center / Subsplash / Breeze:** staff administration tools. "We don't compete — we're the layer that serves the congregation itself. Phase 2 roadmap includes integrating with them."
- **Facebook Groups:** free but algorithmic, ad-driven, no church structure, poor moderation, and members' data is the product. Our line: "No ads. No algorithms. Just the Body, connected."
- **Glorify / Pray.com:** individual devotional apps, not community platforms.
- **"Why not just a GroupMe?"** — no discovery (can't find the roofer you don't know), no profiles, no church page, no insights for leadership, and every ministry runs its own disconnected thread. Groups-as-HQ answers this directly.
- **The moat:** every user is a verified member of a shared church body; once embedded in ministry rhythms (groups, files, calendar), switching costs are steep.

## 1. What the company is (60-second version)

**Interconnected** sells private, invitation-only community platforms to individual churches — one siloed network per congregation where members discover each other's skills, gifts, and needs during the week. Churches subscribe annually ($4,500–$12,000/yr by size); members always join free. Stage: interactive prototype live at **www.joininterconnected.com**, active pastor-feedback campaign in the South Austin / Buda / Kyle corridor, pursuing letters of intent and then funding. Founder: Noah (noah@joininterconnected.com).

**The two-sided value proposition (memorize this — it's the spine of all messaging):**
- *Members join for community* — needs met, gifts discovered, Sunday relationships becoming weekday family.
- *The church pays for community PLUS insight* — the first real visibility any church has ever had into its own body (gifts census, member map, integration funnel, care signals). This second half came from advisor feedback that community alone didn't justify the subscription; it completed the pitch.

## 2. Brand identity system (locked)

**Name:** Interconnected. Formerly "Believers In Business" — renamed by Noah because the product outgrew business networking ("it's not really about business anymore"). The old name must never appear in new materials. Domain: joininterconnected.com (interconnected.com belongs to an unrelated IT firm; "join" was chosen over hyphenated and "get" variants for saying cleanly out loud).

**Logo — the "gathered dot-cross":** many small scattered dots of varying size and blue shade forming a Latin cross silhouette. Meaning: many individual people, gathered into one body, in the shape of the cross — the entire product thesis in one mark. It renders in palette variants per background (blues on white; white/light-blue on the blue gradient; light tones on navy). **The logo's backstory matters for future work:** it emerged from a journey — original node-network mark → brother-in-law feedback ("logo should be a cross with a network element") → nine cross/network concepts rejected → softer/rounded options ("too harsh or sharp" was the critique) → a sparse dot-cross → Noah's request for "a lot more dots" with a defined shape → the current mark. Noah's approval was a lukewarm "cool"; treat the logo as settled-but-revisitable, and never propose changes unprompted.

**Colors:** primary deep blue `#2563EB`; neutrals white / `#F3F4F6` / `#6B7280` / `#1F2937`; navy `#0F1B33` for dark sections; category accents (amber Requests, green `#059669` Offerings, violet `#7C3AED` Questions, blue Announcements). The site opens with a blue gradient flowing from the top into white.

**Typography:** Inter, all weights. Clean, modern, professional.

**Aesthetic law:** *modern but timeless, clean and professional, neutral with one pop color* — Noah's original brand direction, still governing. **No emojis anywhere, ever** (Noah: "this is a professional site"); iconography is a Feather-style SVG line-icon system. Light mode only.

## 3. Voice and tone (follow exactly)

- **Warm, professional, faith-rooted.** The site speaks church natively: "Built by believers, for the local church" · "No ads. No algorithms. Just the Body, connected." · a 1 Peter 4:10 paraphrase ("Every member has a gift. Every gift has a purpose.") · footer "Connecting the Body of Christ — every day of the week." This warmth exists because early versions were criticized as "too techy, not church enough" — but it's balanced deliberately: investors read polish as competence, so warmth was *added to* professionalism, never traded for it.
- **Honest about stage.** The site FAQ openly says prototype-stage, seeking founding pilot churches. Noah is in feedback mode with pastors and refuses bait-and-switch framing. Never overclaim shipped functionality.
- **Never pressuring.** The product philosophy (no gamification, no engagement-farming, no "you should help" nudges) extends to marketing voice. We invite; we don't push. Scarcity tactics stay light ("selecting a small group of founding churches" is the ceiling).
- **Noah's personal writing voice for outreach** (he edits drafts toward this — study it): short sentences, humble, conversational, direct asks, sign-offs like "Much Appreciated" or "Grateful for you." **He strips em dashes from outreach copy** — write emails without them. He softens salesy phrasing ("I'm curious if you are willing to share some wisdom"). When ghostwriting as Noah, draft in this voice, not polished-marketer voice.
- Scripture use: paraphrase with reference, sparingly, never as decoration.

## 4. Messaging architecture

**Tagline:** *Your church, connected every day of the week.*

**Core narrative (the canonical demo story):** Rachel's roof leaks after a storm. Instead of Google, she posts to her church. Marcus, a contractor three pews over, answers in 18 minutes — and James vouches for his work. Marked as helped. That arc anchors the site's guided tour, the pitch deck, and live demos.

**Message pillars:**
1. *A church full of gifts nobody can see* (problem) — anonymous at scale; needs leave the church; six silent days between Sundays.
2. *One private network per church* (solution) — invitation-only, church-branded, fully siloed.
3. *Five tabs, zero pressure* (member product) — feed, member-led groups, search, church page, profile, private encrypted messaging.
4. *Members get community; the church gets stewardship* (buyer product) — insights, 60-second moderation, group oversight, broadcast.
5. *Know your church like never before* (the money pillar) — gifts census, member map, integration funnel, Year in Review for vision night.
6. *Built for church culture, not engagement metrics* (differentiation) — the list of deliberate "no's" competitors can't copy without breaking their models.
7. *The pastor is the channel* (go-to-market) — Launch Sunday, pulpit trust, 50%+ signup thesis; the cold-start problem structurally doesn't apply.
8. *Built to be trusted with a church family* (trust) — encrypted messages, aggregate-only insights, confidential circles, members are never the product.

**Proof points (with sources — always attribute):** 205M+ American Christians (Pew Research) · ~384,000 U.S. churches · 70% of U.S. Christians prefer values-aligned businesses (Barna Group).

**Phrases in the bank:** "turning a big group of strangers into an interconnected community" · "the roofer, the tutor, and the CPA already sitting three pews over" · "shepherding, not surveillance" (insights framing) · "full oversight, minimal burden" (admin promise) · "answered prayers, marked as helped."

## 5. Audiences

1. **Pastors/church leaders (buyer).** Segmented by role: *connections/groups/community pastors* own the exact problem and are the best first conversation; *executive pastors* own operations and budget; *lead pastors* decide at smaller churches (<500) but are poor cold targets at large ones. Target church: 500+ members (sweet spot 1,000–2,000), $10k+ engagement/tech budgets. Current geography: South Austin / Buda / Kyle.
2. **Investors (audience #2).** Faith-driven capital is the wedge: Faith Driven Investor/Entrepreneur, Praxis, OCEAN accelerator. They receive the deck + live demo + (soon) signed LOIs.
3. **Members (end users, not buyers).** Marketing reaches them later through the church itself via the Launch Sunday kit — pulpit script, slides, QR codes. That kit is promised in the deck and FAQ but **does not exist yet**; it's a future flagship deliverable of this workstream.

## 6. Active campaign: pastor feedback outreach (live now)

**Framing doctrine — the most important campaign decision:** outreach asks for *wisdom and feedback*, not sales meetings ("ask for money, get advice; ask for advice, get money" — and it's honestly true at prototype stage). Emails explicitly say "I'm not selling anything."

**The no-link rule:** the demo URL is deliberately withheld from outreach emails. Noah shows the demo in person (member app on his phone, dashboard on a laptop) so he controls first impressions and the demo becomes the reason to meet. The prototype is *mentioned* as the curiosity hook. (The site is still public; a pastor who Googles it will find it — accepted.)

**Materials that exist:** warm and cold email templates in Noah's voice (feedback-first, link-free, em-dash-free); a one-page non-binding LOI with indicative pricing acknowledgment; the 12-slide pitch deck with a scan-the-QR live-demo slide; the site itself as the always-on brochure.

**Personalization doctrine:** first line of every send references something specific about that church (e.g., Austin Stone's member-launched groups philosophy mirroring our product; Gateway's Eric Bryant hosting the Innovative Church Leaders Podcast; The Connection Church literally being named "Connection"). Send Tue–Thu mornings. Follow up once after ~5 days. Every meeting ends with a referral ask.

**Feedback governance:** collect for a week, act on patterns (3+ people), treat single opinions as taste — with exceptions made for strategically compelling insight (the insights value prop came from one person). Noah's wife is the standing taste reviewer (she killed a busy color treatment; her bar is "premium, not busy"). His brother-in-law represents the church-warmth constituency.

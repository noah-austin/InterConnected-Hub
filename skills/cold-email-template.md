# Skill — Cold outreach email (template v3.1)

> **Version in force: v3.1**, adopted Aug 26, 2026. Supersedes v1 (July) and v2 (Aug 24). The description paragraph is Buford's wording and is used **verbatim**; the paragraph order is Noah's. Source: Sonny's Sep 24 handoff §3.
> Agents draft into `work/bd/outbox/`. **Noah sends.** Nothing leaves from a session.

## The template

> **Subject:** Could I get your wisdom on something?
>
> Hey [Name],
>
> Hope you're doing well.
>
> I'm working on a project for churches, and I'm at the stage where I need pastors to tell me honestly what's right, what's wrong, and what a church would never use, before I go any further. I'm not selling anything. There's nothing to buy and no pitch coming; I'm asking for your read.
>
> The short version: it's called Interconnected. One private app for one congregation, built to hold a member's whole church life during the week. Skills and needs, groups, the church calendar, and the people themselves, all in one place. So when someone needs a roofer or a babysitter, the first thought becomes someone at church instead of Google. There's a part built for pastors too.
>
> [Optional: one sentence about their church, only when it's real.] Could I buy you a coffee or grab 20 minutes sometime? I'd show you the prototype on my phone and mostly listen. I'm just looking for someone who shepherds a congregation to tell me where I'm wrong.
>
> Much appreciated,
> Noah
>
> Noah Austin
> Founder, Interconnected
> noah@joininterconnected.com

## Construction rules — do not drift
1. **Order is fixed:** greeting → "working on a project, need honest feedback, not selling" → the description paragraph → the meeting ask. The not-selling framing comes **before** the product is described.
2. **The description paragraph is verbatim.** The four nouns (skills and needs, groups, the church calendar, the people themselves) are never expanded into features or benefits.
3. **The roofer line is canon.** Do not reword it.
4. **"There's a part built for pastors too"** is the entire church-side mention. Insights are shown in the room, never described in email.
5. **Do not open with Noah leading a life group.** He considers it flexing.
6. **No "I'm not looking for you to adopt anything."** It closes the LOI door.
7. **Do not plant a negative the reader hasn't raised** (never "not a surveillance tool").
8. **Ask for feedback on the whole idea**, never steer to one part.
9. **No em dashes. No demo link.**
10. **The personalization sentence goes in only when it's real.** A forced hook reads as research done to sell.
11. **Office-routed addresses** (info@, questions@): put the pastor's name in the subject: "For Pastor [Name] — could I get your wisdom on something?"
12. Competitors unnamed in writing ("a group text and a shared drive"); Planning Center named only as a complement.

## Follow-ups
- **~5 days:** one polite bump, in-thread, 2–3 sentences, no new pitch.
- **A week later:** one final touch.
- **Then** park as Dormant; revisit quarterly.
- **After long silence (weeks):** a **re-approach, not a bump.** One honest note with real news, owning the gap in one sentence, with fresh dates if a meeting had been offered.
